## Context

ADR-014 defines how citizens authenticate to the system through auth-service (sessions, MFA tiers), but nothing defines how the 17+ internal services authenticate to each other. AUTH-010's "system-only permissions" table already lists operations executed only by internal workers, but there is no mechanism proving that a given internal request actually originates from an authorized service. At national scale, inside a Kubernetes cluster with dozens of pods per service, an attacker who reaches the internal network must not be able to impersonate a service — for example, posing as governance-role-service to forge an approval, or as identity-service to fake `citizen.status` as `active` — or silently read internal traffic in transit.

## Decision

Adopt an open-source service mesh (Istio or Linkerd) across every Kubernetes cluster (per ADR-015).

### Workload identity

The mesh issues every service a short-lived cryptographic workload identity following the SPIFFE standard, e.g. `spiffe://digitaldemocracy/ns/{namespace}/sa/{service-account}`. Identities are automatically rotated at 24 hours or less by the mesh's own certificate authority. No service handles or stores its own private key material outside the mesh sidecar.

### Transport

All service-to-service traffic — direct sync calls and Kafka broker connections alike — must use mutual TLS. Default-deny Kubernetes NetworkPolicies reject any plaintext internal traffic, so a connection that cannot present a valid mesh-issued certificate on both ends is dropped at the network layer, not merely rejected at the application layer.

### Authorization

Which service may call which endpoint, or produce/consume which topic, is enforced declaratively:

| Enforcement point | Mechanism | Keyed on |
|---|---|---|
| Sync service-to-service calls | Mesh `AuthorizationPolicy` | SPIFFE identity |
| Kafka topics | Kafka ACLs | SPIFFE identity |

These policies must exactly match the producer/consumer edges already declared in ARCH-005 — no service may call an endpoint or touch a topic not listed there. This mechanism is documented from the permissions side in AUTH-012.

## Consequences

- A compromised pod cannot silently impersonate another service or eavesdrop on internal traffic.
- The ARCH-005 diagrams become an enforced allow-list rather than only documentation — configuration drift away from ARCH-005 becomes a rejected policy violation, not just a stale diagram.
- Adds roughly 1-2ms of sidecar latency per hop and adds mesh certificate rotation and upgrades to the operational surface.
- Scope is limited to internal east-west traffic and the ingress gateway's north-south TLS termination (ARCH-006); citizen-facing authentication under ADR-014 is unaffected.
- Extends AUTH-010's "denied by default" enforcement contract down to the network layer.
