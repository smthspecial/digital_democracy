## Context

ARCH-005 defines roughly twenty named queues (`identity.check`, `identity.revoke`, `proposals.threshold`, `proposals.lifecycle`, `proposals.scope`, `competency.review`, `competency.challenge`, `integrity.coi`, `constitutional.review`, `governance.approvals`, `governance.protocol`, `voting.eligibility`, `voting.tally`, `voting.certify`, `voting.delegation`, `civic.assign`, `reputation.update`, `ai.synthesis`, `audit.append`, `notifications.dispatch`) with a fixed producer/consumer topology, but never named a concrete message-bus technology.

`audit.append` is the special case: every service produces to it, but it has exactly one consumer (audit-service, SRV-012), and that consumer requires exact ordering, not just at-least-once delivery. It feeds an append-only, hash-chained, publicly-auditable log (ADR-006). `notifications.dispatch` is likewise fan-in from every service to a single consumer (notification-service, SRV-015), but tolerates best-effort delivery.

At 10 million citizens the bus must scale horizontally, not just vertically, and must survive individual broker failure without losing queued events.

## Decision

Adopt an open-source, log-based event-streaming platform (Apache Kafka, or a wire-compatible open alternative such as Redpanda) as the backbone for every named queue in ARCH-005 — one Kafka topic per named queue.

Partition topics by `jurisdiction_id` where the message concerns a jurisdiction-scoped entity, otherwise by a hash of `citizen_id` or `proposal_id`, to preserve per-entity ordering while spreading load across partitions.

`audit.append` is the exception: it is either single-partition or strictly key-ordered per audit scope, with infinite retention (compacted plus cold-archived, never deleted) so the hash chain in ADR-006 stays verifiable indefinitely.

All consumer groups implement at-least-once processing with idempotent writes (dedupe keys derived from the source event id), since Kafka does not guarantee exactly-once end to end across service boundaries.

## Consequences

- The bus scales horizontally via partitions, and the durable log gives natural replay — useful for disaster recovery, for bootstrapping a new read-replica, or for rebuilding an audit mirror from scratch.
- Running Kafka at this scale adds operational complexity, mitigated by running it as a Kubernetes-native operator (e.g. Strimzi) alongside the services it serves (see ARCH-006).
- Ordering is only guaranteed within a partition key, so there is no global cross-jurisdiction ordering guarantee. Acceptable because governance invariants (one ballot per token, one vote per session) are enforced at the database/application layer, not by bus ordering alone.
- Realizes NFR-009 and supports NFR-004 (data integrity and immutability).
