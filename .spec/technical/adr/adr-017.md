## Context

NFR-009 (scalability) and NFR-011 (availability and resilience) both require the system to survive national-scale load with an extreme peak-to-average ratio: an ordinary day may see a few thousand concurrent citizens, while a national vote-close deadline can spike to over 100,000 concurrent citizens within a narrow multi-hour window, out of a 10 million registered base. A single-region deployment is simultaneously a scaling ceiling and a single point of failure, which NFR-011 and ADR-001's anti-capture reasoning both treat as unacceptable for infrastructure that carries national governance decisions.

## Decision

Deploy to at least three independent cloud regions (or independent data centers) in an active-active configuration for all stateless and horizontally-scalable components.

### Data layer

Stateful per-service Postgres databases (from ADR-015) run a primary in one region with synchronous replication to a standby in a second region and asynchronous replication to a third, with automated failover.

| Tier | Tables | RTO | RPO |
|------|--------|-----|-----|
| Governance-critical | `ballot`, `audit_log`, `governance_role` | ≤ 5 minutes | ≤ 30 seconds |
| Everything else | All other tables | ≤ 30 minutes | ≤ 5 minutes |

### Autoscaling

Every service's Kubernetes workload uses Horizontal Pod Autoscaler on CPU/memory for request-serving pods, plus KEDA event-driven autoscaling keyed on Kafka consumer-lag for async workers (for example, voting-service tally workers scale on `voting.tally` queue depth). Because vote-session open/close times are scheduled in advance (DP-046, DP-047), capacity is pre-provisioned ahead of known high-load windows via scheduled scale-up, not purely reactive autoscaling.

### Traffic distribution

Traffic is distributed across regions via global anycast/GeoDNS with health-check-based failover at the edge (see ARCH-006).

## Consequences

Significantly higher infrastructure cost and operational complexity, justified because a national governance system cannot go down during a vote. Multi-region deployment raises data-residency questions — some jurisdictions may require in-country storage of citizen data, which needs legal review per jurisdiction in coordination with jurisdiction-service (SRV-002) and is flagged here as an open follow-up, not resolved by this ADR. Read replicas absorb the bulk of read traffic from a 10-million-citizen base while writes stay concentrated to preserve governance invariants (one ballot per token, one active identity per citizen). Realizes NFR-009 and NFR-011; capacity figures are detailed in ARCH-007.
