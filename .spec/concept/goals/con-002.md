## Overview

Defines the twelve goals and the mission behind the digital democracy system. The core aim is to shift citizens from periodic voters into continuous participants in governance, reducing dependence on representation rather than merely improving it. Goals cover direct decision-making, authority proportional to impact, budget transparency and influence, issue-based (not party-based) policy, accountability, continuous participation, collective expertise, local empowerment, anti-corruption, trust, and adaptation to digital-age capabilities.

## Key Points

**Mission Statement**
- Political authority should remain with the people and be exercised directly whenever practical; the objective is to reduce dependence on representation, not just improve it.

**Goal 1: Return Decision-Making to Citizens**
- Shift from a model of political delegation toward a model of political participation, giving citizens direct influence over public decisions.

**Goal 2: Align Authority with Impact**
- Decision-making authority belongs primarily to those affected; the closer a decision is to daily life, the greater the right and responsibility to participate (residents on neighborhoods, cities on local infrastructure, regions on regional transport, nations on national/constitutional matters).

**Goal 3: Make Public Spending Fully Transparent**
- Budgets must be visible, understandable, and traceable, covering revenue sources, fund allocation, approved expenditures, project costs, and outcomes; public money is a shared societal resource, not an abstract government asset.

**Goal 4: Give Citizens Direct Influence Over Budget Priorities**
- Citizens should express collective preferences on spending across infrastructure, education, healthcare, public safety, scientific research, environmental protection, and cultural development; budgets should reflect the priorities of the population funding them.

**Goal 5: Separate Issues from Political Parties**
- Replace party-centered decision-making with proposal-centered decision-making; policy is evaluated issue by issue, and citizens are not forced to accept bundled unrelated policies.

**Goal 6: Increase Accountability**
- Every significant public decision must be traceable: who proposed it, who supported it, resources allocated, expected outcomes, and whether objectives were achieved.

**Goal 7: Create Continuous Governance**
- Participation is not limited to election cycles; citizens can identify problems, submit proposals, discuss, prioritize, and vote on an ongoing basis rather than only periodically.

**Goal 8: Utilize Collective Knowledge**
- Mechanisms should let distributed expertise inform decisions directly, without expertise replacing citizens' democratic authority.

**Goal 9: Strengthen Local Communities**
- Local problems are best understood by those who experience them; the system should let communities identify, discuss, and solve local issues directly, strengthening civic responsibility.

**Goal 10: Reduce Corruption and Waste**
- Transparency, traceability, and public oversight of decisions, budgets, contracts, and outcomes make misuse of public resources easier to detect and harder to conceal.

**Goal 11: Build Trust Through Transparency**
- Trust should come from open processes, visible information, and verifiable outcomes rather than political promises; citizens must be able to independently verify decisions and resource use.

**Goal 12: Establish a Governance System for the Digital Age**
- Representative democracy arose under historical constraints that made large-scale direct participation impossible; modern communication, digital identity, and information networks enable adapting governance to the twenty-first century while preserving stability, rights, and institutional resilience.

**Ultimate Objective**
- Citizens should be active participants in exercising political authority, not merely its theoretical source; the long-term vision is a governance system that is transparent, participatory, accountable, locally responsive, economically visible, and able to evolve with society.
